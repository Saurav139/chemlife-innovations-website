Green Chelation for Smarter Bio-Trace Minerals

Eco-friendly, sustainable mineral chelation

We bind trace minerals to protein from silkworm pupae, a by-product of India's silk industry. A gentle ANBioT process does the bonding, and the result is MinBioZen, a bio-trace mineral for animal feed.

Request the technical dossier How it works
The process

How it works

Chelation holds a mineral inside a ring of amino acids, so it reaches the gut wall in a form the animal can absorb.

  1. Step 1

    Recover the ligand

    Silkworm pupae are left over once silk is reeled from the cocoon. Their protein is rich in hydroxy amino acids, which make good ligands for binding minerals.

  2. Step 2

    Chelate under mild conditions

    A proprietary ANBioT nutrient medium bonds the minerals to those amino acids under milder conditions than conventional chelation. Each mineral ion ends up held in a ring by its ligands.

  3. Step 3

    Deliver it intact

    Inside the ring, the mineral is shielded from compounds in feed, such as phytate, that bind free minerals and stop them being absorbed.

The benefits

Why it matters

  • From waste to ligand

    Gives a silk-industry by-product a second life, a circular-economy step for Indian sericulture.

  • A home-grown alternative

    Replaces imported ligands such as yeast hydrolysate and methionine hydroxy analogue (MHA).

  • Greener chemistry

    Milder reaction conditions, in line with green chemistry principles.
