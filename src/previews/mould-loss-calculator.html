How it works

Feed spoiled = feed produced × share lost to mould × feed value.

Birds fed = feed produced ÷ feed one bird eats (live weight × FCR, allowing for deaths).

Lost in the flocks = flock profit with clean feed − flock profit with this feed. Deaths = general + feed-related + weather-related. Profit per bird = live weight × sale price − cost per bird.

Treat = treatment cost on every tonne, plus whatever spoilage and flock loss it does not stop.

Reject = the part of the feed's value you don't get back. The flocks then eat clean feed. Storage losses stay.

An estimate for planning, not a prediction. Effects are added together, which understates flocks hit by heat and mycotoxins at once. Transport and delay on rejected feed are not counted.

Where the figures come from

  • Storage loss and treatment cost: 10 to 15% lost in unprotected stores and ₹500 to ₹1,000 a tonne, from our Economics of Prevention article. Placeholder The 5% starting figure for a mill is our guess.
  • Mycotoxins, full effect: FCR +0.12, deaths +2.0 points, weight −217 g, applied here as 9% of live weight (Weaver 2022, 25 trials).
  • Mycotoxin level: no effect under 20 ppb, half at 20 to 200 ppb, full above 200 ppb. Our reading of aflatoxin dose trials.
  • Hot season: FCR +0.17, weight −6% (Liu 2020). To check against the full paper. No published figure for deaths, so none is added.
  • Cold season: deaths +7.6 points from ascites (Ipek & Sahan 2006, a severe-cold trial). No published percentage for FCR, so none is added.
  • Acidifier: FCR 3% better (Polycarpo 2017 reports up to 5.7% under disease pressure).
  • Bird figures: FCR 1.80 and 95.3% survival from an Indian field study; dressing yield 67% at about 2.2 kg (IJAS 2024).
  • Placeholder Feed value, other bird costs, how much mycotoxin damage treatment prevents and how much is recovered on rejected feed are our starting guesses, for ChemLife to replace.

Vaccination and antibiotic growth promoters are left out: vaccines protect against deaths only when the disease is present, and antibiotic growth promoters give a small gain and are not for promotion.
